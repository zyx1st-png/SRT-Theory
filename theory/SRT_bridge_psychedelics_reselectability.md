# SRT and psychedelic reselectability

> **Claim level:** bridge hypothesis + lab-facing decomposition. This note does **not** alter the canonical definitions of `L0/L1/L2`, `Ĝθ`, `d-value`, `Ψf`, or `T_dir`. External psychedelic findings may constrain or motivate this bridge, but they do not prove SRT.

## 1. Why this bridge exists

Psychedelic research is useful to SRT not because it shows that "the brain creates reality," and not because a receptor, region, or network can be identified with selection. Its value is more specific: psychedelic perturbations can transiently loosen otherwise stable filtering, routing, self-model, and learning structures, making it easier to observe the difference between **possibility reopening**, **manifest selection**, **re-anchoring**, and **later stabilization**.

The bridge therefore asks:

> When a stabilized `L2` structure loses dominance, what determines whether the system gains real reselectability, merely becomes more labile, or fails to anchor a workable reality at all?

The central distinction is:

$$
\boxed{\text{possibility reopening} \neq \text{reselectability}}
$$

A system may gain access to more candidates without gaining the ability to compare, anchor, test, act on, revise, and responsibly stabilize them.

## 2. Evidence base and boundaries

A 2026 awake-rat mescaline study reported a combination of cerebellar-weighted BOLD changes, widespread functional hyperconnectivity, and frequency-dependent changes in acoustic gating. This is compatible with the idea that psychedelic perturbation can **reconfigure the accessibility landscape** preceding higher-order anchoring. It does **not** establish that the cerebellum is a "reality filter," that BOLD directly measures neural firing, or that the observed rat effects explain human ego dissolution.

Other work provides adjacent but still bounded support for a reopening model. In mice, several psychedelics have been reported to reopen a social-reward learning critical period, suggesting that some stabilized learning windows can become temporarily more plastic. Human psychedelic research also treats preparation, physical environment, therapeutic framework, and post-session context as potentially material to outcome. None of these findings by themselves establish an SRT mechanism.

**Bridge rule:** neural gating, network entropy, critical-period reopening, psychological flexibility, and subjective ego dissolution are all possible **operational proxies or neighboring constructs**. None is identical to SRT selection or reselectability.

## 3. Gating is not selection

Sensory or predictive gating can be modeled as changing which candidate signals remain available for further processing:

$$
\{x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n\}
\xrightarrow{\text{gating}}
\{x_i,x_j,\ldots\}.
$$

SRT selection asks a later and broader question: which candidate becomes manifest, action-coupled, consequential, and stable enough to count as current reality for the system?

A useful process decomposition is therefore:

$$
\boxed{
L0
\rightarrow A_{cand}
\rightarrow \text{selection}
\rightarrow L1\text{ anchoring}
\rightarrow \text{resistance / consequence}
\rightarrow L2\text{ deposition}
}
$$

where `A_cand` is **candidate accessibility**. This decomposition is bridge notation, not a new canonical ontology.

A gating perturbation may alter `A_cand`; it does not by itself determine what becomes `L1`, what survives resistance, or what later hardens into `L2`.

## 4. Bounded reselectability

The psychedelic case makes visible a distinction that generalizes beyond psychedelics:

$$
R_s \neq A_{cand}
$$

where `R_s` denotes **reselectability** in this note: the system's capacity to leave an over-dominant prior selection, keep alternatives genuinely live, anchor a new selection, receive the consequences of that selection, and retain the ability to revise again when conditions change.

A provisional lab-facing decomposition is:

$$
R_s = f(A_{cand}, S_{anchor}, C_{return}, T_{dir}, P_{L2})
$$

with:

- `A_cand` — how many relevant candidates remain genuinely accessible;
- `S_anchor` — whether a selected candidate can remain stable enough for action and evaluation;
- `C_return` — whether consequences return to the same bearer rather than being absorbed elsewhere;
- `T_dir` — whether the system can read the direction of its own selection strongly enough to reorient;
- `P_L2` — whether a useful selection can deposit into future structure without becoming unrevisable lock-in.

This equation is **not canonical**. It is an experimental decomposition whose value depends on whether the components can be operationalized without collapsing into existing constructs such as salience, confidence, reward, cognitive flexibility, or generic task performance.

## 5. Over-hardening and under-anchoring

SRT already treats excessive stabilization as dangerous when a structure stops reducing friction for future choice and instead replaces live choice. Psychedelic perturbation exposes the mirror problem: **too little anchoring can also destroy usable selectability**.

The candidate landscape is therefore bounded:

$$
\boxed{
\text{over-hardening}
\leftrightarrow
\text{adaptive reselectability}
\leftrightarrow
\text{under-anchoring}
}
$$

### Over-hardening

A stabilized `L2` structure dominates so strongly that alternatives cannot re-enter live competition. Possible behavioral analogues include compulsive repetition, rigid self-schema, addiction-like attractors, or inflexible habit loops.

### Adaptive reselectability

The system is:

> **stable enough to act, open enough to revise.**

Alternatives can re-enter when evidence, cost, or consequences warrant it; new selections can still be anchored and tested.

### Under-anchoring

Candidate accessibility is high but anchoring is weak. The system may switch rapidly among meanings or interpretations, fail to maintain action-guiding commitments, or grant transient candidates disproportionate reality weight before resistance and convergence can test them.

The bridge therefore rejects both:

$$
\text{maximum stability} = \text{health}
$$

and:

$$
\text{maximum plasticity} = \text{freedom}.
$$

## 6. Ψf and the payable-friction window

This bridge gives a concrete interpretation to an existing SRT boundary: zero friction is not the goal.

Too much friction can make revision prohibitively costly:

$$
\Psi_f \uparrow \Rightarrow \text{lock-in risk}.
$$

But friction that becomes too low can make candidate replacement too cheap:

$$
\Psi_f \downarrow\downarrow \Rightarrow \text{instability / weak reality testing risk}.
$$

The adaptive region is therefore hypothesized to require a **payable but non-zero** friction window. This is a structural hypothesis, not a claim that `Ψf` is a simple scalar with an already-established U-shaped biological curve.

## 7. Bearer–niche re-anchoring

Psychedelic research also pressures brain-only accounts because the same pharmacological perturbation can unfold differently across bodily state, expectation, history, interpersonal context, and physical environment.

For bridge purposes only, current manifestation may be written relationally as:

$$
L1_t = \hat G_{\theta_t}(L0_t \mid B_t \leftrightarrow N_t),
$$

where:

- `B_t` is the current bearer — the system that actually carries the state, risk, memory, and returning consequences;
- `N_t` is the enacted niche — the relevant physical, social, and normative environment in which selection is occurring.

This notation does **not** replace the canonical definition of `Ĝθ`. It makes explicit a consequence already implicit in `θ`: environment is not always an external decoration on an internal model; it can be part of the constraint field through which a selection becomes actionable and maintainable.

This matters most during reopening. A destabilized `L2` does not specify what comes next. Re-anchoring occurs through bearer–niche interaction, not through pharmacology alone.

## 8. Ego dissolution: three non-identities

This bridge also imposes a boundary on psychedelic selfhood claims:

$$
\boxed{
\text{ego dissolution}
\neq
\text{subject dissolution}
\neq
\text{bearer dissolution}
}
$$

A psychedelic state may weaken autobiographical identity, agency, body ownership, self-location, or the ordinary binding among them. That does not by itself establish that subjecthood literally disappears, and it does not dissolve the bearer that continues to metabolize the drug, carry risk, form memory, and receive later consequences.

This is consistent with SRT's core reversal that the subject is not a primitive chooser prior to selection, but a stabilized result of sufficiently self-referential selection. Psychedelic perturbation is therefore potentially useful as a way to study **how a stable subject-position can partially decompose**, not as proof that subjects are unreal.

## 9. A four-phase reopening model

For experiments and clinical translation, the bridge proposes four phases:

### Phase A — destabilization

An over-dominant `L2_old` loses some control over current manifestation.

### Phase B — reopening

Previously suppressed or inaccessible candidates become more available:

$$
A_{cand} \uparrow.
$$

### Phase C — re-selection

Candidates compete under a temporarily altered operator and current bearer–niche constraints:

$$
\{A,B,C,\ldots\}
\xrightarrow{\hat G_{\theta'}}
L1'.
$$

### Phase D — re-deposition

A new selection is repeated, acted on, exposed to resistance, and—if it remains payable and useful—deposits into future structure:

$$
L1' \rightarrow L2'.
$$

The therapeutic or adaptive target is therefore **not maximal acute disruption**. It is a new stabilized structure that preserves more future re-entry into live choice than the structure it replaces:

$$
\boxed{R_s(L2') > R_s(L2_{old})}.
$$

## 10. Integration as re-anchoring: hypothesis, not established fact

In this bridge, post-acute integration is interpreted more strongly than "making sense of an experience." Its candidate role is to help new `L1` selections encounter action, bodily feedback, social reality, contradiction, and consequence before they harden into `L2`.

That yields the proposed chain:

$$
L1_{new}
\rightarrow
\text{action}
\rightarrow
\text{resistance}
\rightarrow
\text{consequence}
\rightarrow
L2'.
$$

This is a **bridge hypothesis**. Existing psychedelic-assisted therapy trials often bundle preparation, drug administration, support, and integration, so the independent causal contribution of integration remains incompletely isolated.

## 11. Discriminating prediction

A useful SRT bridge must predict something stronger than "psychedelics increase flexibility." Predictive-processing, REBUS-style, critical-period, and psychological-flexibility accounts can already motivate that direction.

The SRT-specific candidate is:

> **Long-term adaptive outcome should depend on the interaction between reopening and successful re-anchoring, not on acute destabilization alone.**

Compare:

$$
M_1: Outcome \sim AcuteIntensity
$$

$$
M_2: Outcome \sim PsychologicalFlexibility
$$

$$
M_3^{SRT}: Outcome \sim Opening + Reanchoring + Opening\times Reanchoring + ConsequenceReturn.
$$

A stronger version predicts a bounded relation:

- too little destabilization → insufficient revision;
- moderate reopening + successful re-anchoring → adaptive reselectability;
- excessive destabilization + weak re-anchoring → poor stabilization or maladaptive deposition.

## 12. Falsification and downgrade conditions

This bridge should be weakened or retired if any of the following persist under preregistered testing:

1. **No incremental value.** Reopening × re-anchoring predicts no outcome variance beyond established constructs such as symptom change, psychological flexibility, salience, expectancy, therapeutic alliance, or generic learning.
2. **No bounded region.** Outcome improves monotonically with destabilization/plasticity across well-measured ranges, with no evidence that anchoring quality matters.
3. **No consequence-return increment.** Measures intended to capture bearer-bound consequence return add nothing beyond reward, confidence, arousal, or task performance.
4. **No distinction between access and anchoring.** Candidate accessibility and manifest/action-coupled stabilization cannot be empirically separated.
5. **No subject-position decomposition.** Self-model components do not dissociate in ways that justify treating subject stabilization as a layered process.

Failure here does not refute all of SRT. It specifically weakens the psychedelic / bounded-reselectability bridge.

## 13. What this note does not claim

- Mescaline does not prove SRT.
- Cerebellar gating is not identical to selection.
- BOLD suppression is not identical to reduced neural firing.
- Psychedelic entropy is not identical to `L0`, possibility, or freedom.
- Critical-period reopening is not identical to human personality reopening.
- Ego dissolution does not prove that subjects do not exist.
- Psychological flexibility is not identical to SRT reselectability.
- Integration is not yet established as the unique or necessary mediator of psychedelic benefit.
- More plasticity is not automatically better.

## 15. Compressed takeaway

Psychedelic perturbation is useful to SRT mainly as a stress test of stabilization. It suggests a research program in which **over-hardening** and **under-anchoring** are treated as mirror failures, and adaptive freedom is neither maximal rigidity nor maximal openness. The distinctive SRT question is not how many possibilities a system can generate, but whether it can reopen a settled reality, anchor a replacement under real constraints, receive the consequences, and still preserve the capacity to choose again.
